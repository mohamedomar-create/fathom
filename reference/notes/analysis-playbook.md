# Analysis playbook: reading each page and writing commentary

Write for a business owner. Short sentences, plain words, numbers with a comparison. Each comment: **answer, evidence, so-what, action.**

## Executive summary
Open with the single most important thing. Not a recap of the numbers. Examples:
- "Profit held up but cash did not: EBIT was EGP 162K, yet operating cash flow was negative because receivables grew EGP 220K."
- "Three consecutive months of growth; margin pressure is now the issue, with gross margin down 2.1 points."
Then at most three actions, each with an owner or a date if known.

## Page by page
| Page | Read it for | Typical comment |
|---|---|---|
| KPIs | Which Critical KPIs are red; whether red ones share a cause | "Four of the five red KPIs trace to slow collections." |
| KPI Explorer | Share on track; the cluster of red squares | Use only to support the KPI comment |
| Profitability | Margin of safety (how far revenue can fall before losses); variable cost per 1 of sales | "Revenue can drop 42% before we lose money. Fixed costs are not the problem; margin is." |
| Cash flow | Where cash came from and went. Big bars outside the profit lines (receivables, inventory, payables, capex) | "EGP 380K went into stock. That is a purchasing decision, not trading performance." |
| Financials | Variances above 10%, balance sheet shape, common-size anomalies | Pick the 2 or 3 largest moves; ignore noise |
| Trend | Direction over 6 to 12 months, not one month; seasonality | "Revenue is flat since March; margin target missed 5 of the last 6 months." |
| Growth | Quadrant: Efficiency gains (more profit with less capital), Quality growth (more profit with more capital), Decline, Stress (more capital, less profit) | Stress is the one to call out |
| Goalseek | The one lever that reaches the target with the smallest change | "A 1.8% price rise alone reaches the 15% target; cutting fixed costs needs 7.4%." |

## Relationships that matter most
- **Profit vs cash.** Profit up and cash down means working capital (receivables, stock) is absorbing it. Check AR days and inventory days first.
- **Margin vs volume.** Revenue up with margin down can mean discounting or mix shift; always check both.
- **Fixed-cost leverage.** High fixed costs make good months very good and bad months painful. Quote the margin of safety.
- **Liquidity trap.** Current ratio under 1 or interest cover under 1.5 is a warning even if profit is fine.
- **Cash conversion cycle.** AR days + stock days - supplier days = days the business funds itself.

## Honesty rules
- Separate **what the numbers show** from **what might explain it**. Explanations you cannot prove go in as "Question for management".
- Do not praise by default. If the quarter is good, say what drove it and whether it is repeatable.
- State data limits (few months of history, default targets, plugged equity) where they affect a conclusion.
- No jargon without a plain-words gloss the first time (EBIT = profit before interest and tax).

## Length
Summary: headline sentence plus 3 actions. Section comments: 2 to 4 sentences. If a section has nothing worth saying, leave it to the auto notes or omit.
